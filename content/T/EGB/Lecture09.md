# Protein production and genetic engineering in Agriculture

This lecture addresses the production of proteins from cloned genes and
the application of gene cloning and DNA analysis in agriculture. It covers
strategies for heterologous gene expression in bacterial, yeast, and
eukaryotic systems, including the choice of expression vectors, promoters,
fusion tags, and host strains, as well as factors influencing protein yield,
solubility, and stability. Methods for optimizing expression, such as codon
optimization, control of induction conditions, and subcellular targeting,
are presented alongside approaches for protein purification and functional
verification. The lecture also examines the use of gene cloning and DNA
analysis in agriculture, focusing on the identification and manipulation of
genes underlying economically important traits, the generation of
genetically modified organisms, and the use of molecular markers, DNA
fingerprinting, and transgenic technologies in crop and livestock
improvement. Ethical, environmental, and regulatory considerations
associated with agricultural biotechnology are introduced to contextualize
these applications.
