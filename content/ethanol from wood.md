# Ethanol from wood: yield versus theoretical

## Problem

The first ethanol-from-cellulose plant (Germany, 1898) used dilute acid to hydrolyze cellulose to glucose.
It managed to produce 7.6 L of 100% ethanol per 100 kg of wood. We can assume that 50 % of the wood is
cellulose and that this can be hydrolyzed completely to same mass of glucose. The density of ethanol is  $0.789\ \text{kg/L}$

$M_\text{glucose} = 180\ \text{g/mol}$; $M_\text{EtOH} = 46\ \text{g/mol}$.

**Question:** how far is this yield compared with the theoretical yield?

## Solution

**1. Reaction.** Fermentation of glucose gives two ethanol and two CO₂ per glucose:

$\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 \longrightarrow 2,C_2H_5OH + 2,CO_2}$

**2. Glucose available.** Per 100 kg of wood we have 50 kg glucose:

$m_\text{glucose} = 0.50 \times 100\ \text{kg} = 50\ \text{kg} = 50\times10^{3}\ \text{g}$

$n_\text{glucose} = \frac{50\times10^{3}\ \text{g}}{180\ \text{g/mol}} = 277.8\ \text{mol}$

**3. Theoretical ethanol.** Fermentation yields two moles of ethanol per mole of glucose:

$n_\text{EtOH} = 2 \times 277.8\ \text{mol} = 555.6\ \text{mol}$

$m_\text{EtOH} = 555.6\ \text{mol} \times 46\ \text{g/mol} = 25.56\times10^{3}\ \text{g} = 25.6\ \text{kg}$

**4. Actual ethanol.** The reported volume expressed as mass:

$m_\text{EtOH} = 7.6\ \text{L} \times 0.789\ \text{kg/L} = 6.0\ \text{kg}$

**5. Yield.**

$\text{Yield} = \frac{6.0\ \text{kg}}{25.6\ \text{kg}} \approx 0.235 = 23.5\% $

> **Answer:** the 1898 process reached about 23.5% of the theoretical yield.

## Discussion

- The theoretical maximum is $\dfrac{2 \times 46}{180} = 0.51$ g ethanol per g glucose. The process gave about 0.12 g per g of glucose-equivalent.
- Possible reasons for the low yield: incomplete hydrolysis of cellulose, degradation of glucose by hot acid, sugars from hemicellulose and lignin-derived inhibitors not handled, incomplete fermentation, and losses in distillation. Biomass used for growth and by-products (glycerol, acetic acid) also lowers the yield.
- Even in modern fermentations the practical limit is about 90–95% of theoretical, because some glucose supports cell growth and by-product formation.
